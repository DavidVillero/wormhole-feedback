What is being computed

  • The full Einstein equations for a spherically symmetric spacetime holding two fields: the exotic (phantom) field that keeps the Ellis-Bronnikov wormhole open, and ordinary matter. Your browser solves them in real time on a grid of 3,600 points out to 90 throat radii, and checks the Einstein constraints as it goes.
  • This is the same code we validated in Python: it reproduces those runs to a few parts in a billion, pulse for pulse.
  • Every pulse, from the controller or from you, is added as a shell of field with the geometry re-solved around it, so the spacetime always satisfies Einstein's equations.
  • The controller is the Kalman filter and regulator from our tests. It sees only the throat's radius and how fast it is changing, delayed by the light-travel time, and it ignores the first 10 time units, which carry a start-up transient.
  • Lengths are in throat radii; times are light-crossing times of the throat. The drawing is the shape space would have if it could be embedded in flat space, with small changes magnified so you can see them.

How far away can the controller sit?

The further away it is, the longer the delay, and the more exactly it must know how the throat responds. Each extra throat radius of distance tightens the requirement about tenfold.

Here the grid itself is a source of model error: the controller was calibrated on this same grid, but the slow numerical drift of the wormhole still counts against its margin. In our tests that is enough to defeat it at distance 2 and beyond.

Limits: spherical symmetry only (the throat can only breathe), perfect sensors and pulses, and phantom matter with negative energy, which nothing says exists. When a singularity forms the solver has to stop.
