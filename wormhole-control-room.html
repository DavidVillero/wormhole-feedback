What this is

  • The throat follows a model fitted to our full Einstein-scalar simulations of the Ellis-Bronnikov wormhole: one unstable mode that grows by a factor of 3.26 per throat radius of time, a permanent shift in radius left by every pulse, and the measured transient in between.
  • The controller is the Kalman filter and regulator we tested in those simulations, with the same tuning, reading the throat radius and its rate of change after a light-travel delay.
  • Lengths are in throat radii and times in light-crossing times of the throat. One pulse can be launched every quarter of a time unit.
  • The model is linear, so it is only trustworthy while the throat stays near rest. Once the offset passes 30% the run is called lost and the collapse or blow-out you see is an illustration, not a calculation.
  • Pulses of both signs need "phantom" matter with negative energy. Nothing here says such matter exists.

How far away can the controller sit?

The further away it is, the longer the delay, and the more exactly it must know how the throat responds. Each extra throat radius of distance tightens the requirement about tenfold.
